Pools in Boca Raton

Two towns, one city

Boca is really two pool towns. East of Federal the pools are older, mostly open to the sky, and close enough to the ocean that salt gets into everything. West of Military they're newer, almost all screened, and sitting under enough ficus and live oak to fill a basket in three days.

Those two halves need different work. An east Boca pool needs the equipment watched for corrosion and the chemistry held tighter against evaporation and wind. A west Boca screened pool needs the cage tracks and the waterline brushed properly, because the debris that gets past the screen settles in the same corners every week. We run both ends of the city and quote them differently, because pretending they're the same job is how a pool ends up neglected.

Local know-how

The three things that go wrong here

1

Screen cages and debris

West Boca cages trap what blows in and then hold it. Baskets fill faster than owners expect, and a full pump basket is the most common cause of a pool that looks fine while its motor quietly runs hot.

2

Salt systems

Salt cells are close to standard in Boca now. They need inspecting and cleaning on a schedule or they stop producing chlorine while the panel still reports everything is fine.

3

HOA rules

Plenty of Boca communities set rules on equipment noise, pump run times and what can sit on the pad. We work inside them rather than finding out when a letter arrives.

Pricing in Boca Raton

Quoted on your pool

Boca runs the full range. A compact screened pool in Boca Del Mar and a big open pool east of Federal aren't the same monthly rate, and pretending otherwise is how people end up underserviced.

So we quote the pool, not the zip code. Weekly plans start at $129 a month and move up with size, equipment and how much chemical work the water actually needs.
