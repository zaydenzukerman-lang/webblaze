Health Insurance for Self-Employed Individuals: A Practical Guide

6 min read · Updated July 21, 2026

Being self-employed gives you control over your work, but it also means building your own benefits package. Without an employer selecting and subsidizing a plan, you must decide which coverage fits your household, business income, and medical needs.

The good news is that self-employed individuals may have several coverage paths.

1. ACA Marketplace coverage

The Health Insurance Marketplace offers ACA-compliant individual and family plans. Eligibility for financial assistance is based on factors such as projected household income, household size, and access to other qualifying coverage.

Income can fluctuate when you own a business or work on contract. Use a realistic annual estimate and update the Marketplace if your expected income changes. Keeping the estimate current may reduce surprises when you file your federal tax return.

Marketplace coverage is often a strong starting point if you:

  • Qualify for premium tax credits
  • Have a pre-existing condition
  • Need maternity or comprehensive prescription benefits
  • Want ACA-compliant major medical coverage

2. Coverage directly from a carrier

Some insurance companies offer individual coverage outside the Marketplace. An off-Marketplace ACA plan still follows ACA consumer protections, but it does not come with Marketplace financial assistance.

Other private products may not be ACA compliant and may use medical underwriting, exclusions, waiting periods, or fixed benefit schedules. Review the policy carefully so you understand what the plan pays and what remains your responsibility.

3. Coverage through a spouse

If your spouse has employer-sponsored coverage, compare the cost of joining that plan against buying your own. Review the employer contribution for dependents, the network, deductibles, and total family out-of-pocket exposure.

Access to a spouse's employer plan may affect eligibility for Marketplace savings depending on affordability and other rules.

4. Small-group health insurance

If your company has eligible employees, a small-group plan may be an option. Participation and employer-contribution requirements vary by carrier and state.

Business owners should be ready to provide information such as:

  • Business location and industry
  • Employee census
  • Employee eligibility and hours
  • Employer contribution
  • Desired effective date
  • Current coverage information, if applicable

Small-group coverage can help with recruiting and retention, but it creates an ongoing employer expense. Compare both the business cost and the employee experience.

5. COBRA or continuation coverage

If you recently left a job, COBRA may let you keep the same employer plan temporarily. The major advantage is continuity: you may keep the same network and benefits. The downside is cost because you may become responsible for the full premium plus an administrative fee.

Before declining COBRA, compare its benefits and effective date with every alternative. If you are in active treatment, continuity may be worth more than a lower premium.

Build the plan around your real life

Self-employed professionals should evaluate:

  • Expected annual income
  • Preferred doctors and hospitals
  • Prescription medications
  • Planned procedures
  • Maternity needs
  • Frequency of travel
  • Emergency savings
  • Ability to handle a deductible
  • Need for nationwide or out-of-network access

Someone with substantial savings may accept a higher deductible to lower the premium. Someone expecting regular treatment may benefit from stronger copays or lower cost sharing.

Keep business and personal planning connected

Ask a tax professional whether health insurance premiums may be deductible in your situation. Tax treatment depends on individual facts, business structure, and current law.

Also set a yearly coverage-review date. Networks, premiums, prescriptions, income, and family needs can change. A plan that worked this year may not remain the best option next year.

The bottom line

Self-employed health insurance should protect both your health and your cash flow. Start with comprehensive coverage needs, check financial-assistance eligibility, confirm providers, and compare the total annual cost.
