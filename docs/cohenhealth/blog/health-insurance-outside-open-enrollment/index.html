Can You Get Health Insurance Outside Open Enrollment?

6 min read · Updated July 27, 2026

Missing Open Enrollment does not always mean you must remain uninsured. Depending on your circumstances, you may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, Medicaid or CHIP, employer coverage, or another available form of coverage.

The correct path depends on why you need insurance, when the change occurred, your household income, and the rules in your state.

What is Open Enrollment?

Open Enrollment is the annual period when eligible consumers can enroll in or change an individual ACA Marketplace plan without needing a qualifying life event. Dates and deadlines can change by year and may differ on state-based Marketplaces.

Because effective dates and document deadlines matter, verify the current schedule on the official Marketplace used in your state.

What is a Special Enrollment Period?

A Special Enrollment Period, or SEP, is a limited opportunity to enroll after certain qualifying events. Common events can include:

  • Losing qualifying health coverage
  • Getting married
  • Having a baby, adopting a child, or receiving a foster-care placement
  • Moving and meeting applicable prior-coverage rules
  • Certain changes in household status
  • Certain changes affecting Marketplace eligibility

Not every loss of coverage qualifies. Voluntarily canceling a plan or losing coverage because premiums were not paid generally does not create the same rights as an involuntary loss of qualifying coverage.

Documents you may need

The Marketplace may request proof of the event. Depending on your situation, documentation can include:

  • A coverage termination letter
  • A COBRA notice
  • A marriage certificate
  • Proof of a previous and new address
  • Birth or adoption documents
  • Employer correspondence

Submit accurate documents promptly. Missing a verification deadline can delay or prevent enrollment.

Medicaid and CHIP

Medicaid and the Children's Health Insurance Program generally accept applications throughout the year. Eligibility depends on factors such as income, household size, age, pregnancy, disability status, and state rules.

Children may qualify for Medicaid or CHIP even when their parents do not. If your income changes, it may be worth checking eligibility again.

Employer-sponsored coverage

Starting a new job may give you an opportunity to enroll in the employer's plan. Employers can impose a waiting period within legal limits, so ask when coverage begins and what enrollment deadline applies.

Marriage, birth, adoption, or loss of other coverage may also create a special enrollment right under an employer plan. Contact the benefits administrator quickly because employer deadlines may be short.

COBRA and continuation coverage

COBRA may allow eligible employees and dependents to continue an employer plan after certain events. It can preserve the same network and benefits, but the individual may have to pay the full cost.

There are strict election and payment rules. Review the official notice carefully. Do not assume that voluntarily dropping COBRA will always create a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period.

Other private coverage

Depending on the state, private products may be available outside Open Enrollment. These can include short-term medical, medically underwritten, limited-benefit, or supplemental products.

These products may not provide ACA-compliant major medical coverage. They may exclude pre-existing conditions, cap benefits, omit important services, or use fixed payment schedules. Ask for the official policy and understand what is not covered.

What should you do first?

  1. Identify the date your prior coverage ended or will end.
  2. Determine whether the loss was voluntary or involuntary.
  3. Gather proof of the qualifying event.
  4. Check Marketplace, Medicaid, employer, and COBRA eligibility.
  5. Compare effective dates so you understand any possible gap.
  6. Review any private alternative carefully before enrolling.

Time matters. Many enrollment opportunities remain available only for a limited period before or after an event.

The bottom line

You may be able to obtain health insurance outside Open Enrollment, but the reason and timing determine your choices. Acting early gives you more time to provide documents, compare plans, and avoid an unnecessary coverage gap.
