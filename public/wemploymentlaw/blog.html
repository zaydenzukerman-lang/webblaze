Wrongful Termination

Were You Wrongfully Terminated in California?

California is an "at-will" employment state, which means an employer can generally end the working relationship at any time. But "at-will" has real limits — and firing you for the wrong reason is illegal.

When a firing crosses the line

  • You were let go because of a protected characteristic — race, sex, age, disability, religion, national origin, or pregnancy.
  • You were fired in retaliation for reporting harassment, discrimination, safety issues, or wage violations.
  • You were terminated for taking legally protected leave, or for refusing to do something illegal.

If any of those sound familiar, the "at-will" label doesn't protect your employer. A free case review is the fastest way to find out whether what happened to you was unlawful.

Wages & Overtime

California Overtime: Are You Being Paid What You're Owed?

California's overtime rules are more generous than federal law — and that's exactly why they're so often violated. Most non-exempt employees are entitled to extra pay well before they hit 40 hours in a week.

The basics most employers get wrong

  • 1.5× pay for hours over 8 in a single day, or over 40 in a week.
  • 2× pay (double-time) for hours over 12 in a day.
  • Off-the-clock work — answering messages, prep, or cleanup — still counts as time worked.
  • Being paid a salary does not automatically make you "exempt" from overtime.

Harassment

What Counts as Workplace Harassment — and What to Do

Harassment becomes illegal when it's based on a protected characteristic and is severe or pervasive enough to create a hostile work environment. A single serious incident can qualify; so can a steady pattern of smaller ones.

Steps that protect you

  • Write down what happened — dates, times, what was said, and who witnessed it.
  • Report it in writing through your employer's process, if you safely can.
  • Keep copies of messages, emails, and any relevant documents.

Your employer has a legal duty to prevent and correct harassment. If they ignored it — or punished you for reporting it — you may have a claim. Everything you share with us stays confidential.

Leave & Pregnancy

Your Rights During Pregnancy and Medical Leave

California offers some of the strongest leave protections in the nation. Depending on your situation and employer size, you may be entitled to protected time off for pregnancy, a serious health condition, or to care for a family member — with the right to return to your job.

What that can include

  • Protected leave for pregnancy-related disability and bonding with a new child.
  • Reasonable accommodations for pregnancy, such as modified duties.
  • Protection from being demoted or fired for taking leave you're entitled to.

If you were denied leave, pushed out, or treated differently after announcing a pregnancy or health condition, that may be unlawful. A free review will tell you where you stand.

Misclassification

Employee or Independent Contractor? Why It Matters

Some employers label workers as "independent contractors" to avoid paying overtime, providing breaks, or covering expenses. California uses a strict test to decide who is really an employee — and the label your employer used isn't the deciding factor.

Signs you may be misclassified

  • Your employer controls how, when, and where you do your work.
  • The work you do is a core part of the company's normal business.
  • You don't independently run your own separate business doing that work.

If you've been misclassified, you may be owed unpaid overtime, missed-break pay, and reimbursement for work expenses. It costs nothing to find out.

Retaliation

Can My Employer Retaliate If I Speak Up?

In California, it is illegal for an employer to punish you for asserting your rights — such as reporting harassment or discrimination, filing a wage complaint, or raising a safety concern. Retaliation can take many forms beyond being fired.

What retaliation can look like

  • Termination, demotion, or a sudden cut in hours or pay.
  • Being reassigned to worse shifts or duties after you complained.
  • Increased scrutiny, write-ups, or harassment following a report.

If something changed for the worse after you spoke up, the timing matters — and it may be unlawful. Talk to us, free and in confidence.
