1099 vs. W2: How Your Pay Structure Changes Your Health Benefits

5 min read · Updated October 17, 2026

You can do the exact same driving job as the person parked next to you and end up with a completely different health benefits situation, depending on one thing: whether you're classified as a W2 employee or a 1099 independent contractor.

W2 company drivers

As a W2 employee, you're usually eligible for your carrier's group health plan, sometimes after a waiting period — 30, 60, or 90 days is common for a new hire. The employer typically shares the premium cost with you, enrollment happens during the company's open enrollment window or when you're first hired, and if you leave, COBRA lets you continue that same plan for a period, at full price.

1099 owner-operators and independent contractors

As a 1099 contractor, none of that applies. There's no group plan to join and no employer premium share — you're fully responsible for finding and paying for your own coverage. The upside: you're not limited to whatever single plan your carrier happened to offer. You can compare any plan, any network, and deduct the premiums as a business expense in many cases.

The classification itself isn't something you pick

Whether you're legally a W2 employee or a 1099 contractor depends on how the work actually operates — who controls your schedule and routes, who owns the equipment, how you're paid — not just what's written on a form. If you think you might be misclassified, that's a question for an employment or tax professional, not something this article can settle for you.

What doesn't change either way

Your actual health needs don't care about your tax classification. DOT physical requirements apply the same way to W2 and 1099 drivers alike, and neither classification changes what a health plan is actually supposed to cover you for.

Common questions

I just went from company driver to owner-operator — how fast do I need new coverage? As soon as possible. COBRA from your old job is an option, but it's usually the most expensive one since you lose the employer's share of the premium.

Can a 1099 driver get the same kind of plan a company driver has? Often better — without being limited to one carrier's single group offering, you can shop private PPO and Marketplace plans side by side and pick what actually fits.
