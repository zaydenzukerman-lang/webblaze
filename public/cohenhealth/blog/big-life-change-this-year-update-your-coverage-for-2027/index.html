Big Life Change This Year? Update Your Coverage for 2027

6 min read · Updated October 21, 2026

A lot can happen in a year. Maybe you got married, welcomed a baby, moved to a new town, started your own business, or watched a grown child pack up for college. Life moves fast, and health insurance has a way of staying stuck where you left it.

That matters right now. As fall turns toward the end of the year, many people are getting ready to look at their options for 2027. If your life looks different than it did when you last picked a plan, this is a great moment to make sure your coverage matches the life you actually live today.

This guide walks through the most common life changes, what each one can mean for your health plan, and simple steps to get things lined up for the new year.

Why Life Changes Matter for Your Health Plan

Your health plan was built around a snapshot of your life at the time you enrolled. It was based on who lived in your home, where you lived, how much your household expected to earn, and which doctors you saw.

When any of those things change, a few things can happen:

  • Your plan may no longer cover the right people.
  • Your doctors or nearby hospitals may not be in your plan's network anymore.
  • If you get financial help through the ACA Marketplace, the amount could be off, since it is often tied to household size and income.
  • A different type of plan might simply fit your new situation better.

The good news is that you do not have to figure this out alone. The key is noticing what changed and making sure it gets reflected in your coverage.

Common Life Changes and What They Can Mean

Here are some of the most common changes people experience in a year, and what to think about for each one.

Getting married. When two people join households, they often have choices. You might each keep your own coverage, move onto one plan together, or pick a brand-new family plan. The right answer depends on what each of you has access to, your doctors, and how you each use care. If you buy Marketplace coverage, your household size and combined income both count, so it is worth reviewing.

Having or adopting a baby. A new child is one of the biggest reasons to review coverage. You will want to make sure your little one is added to a plan, and you may want to look closely at pediatricians in the network, well-child visits, and what your plan's family deductible looks like.

Divorce or separation. Coverage often needs to be split up. If you were on a spouse's plan, you may need your own. Children's coverage may also need to be sorted out.

Moving to a new area. Health plans are often built around local networks. A plan that worked great in one county or state may not work well in another, or may not be offered there at all. A move is a strong reason to shop again.

A grown child moving away. If your child is heading to college or a job in another state, check whether your plan's network reaches where they live now. Some plans work mostly in one area, while others offer broader national networks.

Starting a business or going freelance. Leaving a job with employer benefits to work for yourself means finding your own coverage. Your income may also change from month to month, which can affect Marketplace financial help.

Income going up or down. A raise, job loss, or new side income can all change what you qualify for on the Marketplace. Keeping your income estimate updated helps avoid surprises later.

Do You Have to Wait Until Open Enrollment?

Not always. Many people think they can only change plans during the yearly open enrollment window. That window is a great time to review and switch, but certain life events may also let you make changes at other times of the year. This is often called a special enrollment period.

Events like getting married, having a baby, adopting a child, losing other coverage, or moving to a new area can sometimes qualify. The rules, time limits, and proof you may need vary by situation, by state, and by the type of plan. Some events give you only a short window to act, so it pays not to wait.

If you had a life change earlier this year and never updated your coverage, it is still worth talking it through. An advisor can help you see whether you can make a change now, or whether the upcoming enrollment period for 2027 is your best chance.

Also keep in mind that private plans, like some PPO options, can have their own enrollment rules separate from the Marketplace. Those details vary, so it helps to ask.

A Simple Checklist to Get Ready for 2027

Before you look at plans for next year, take a few minutes to walk through this list. It will make comparing options much easier.

  1. Write down every person who will need coverage next year, including new family members and anyone who has moved out.
  2. Note your current address and any address changes for people on your plan, such as a student living elsewhere.
  3. Make your best estimate of your household income for 2027, especially if it changed this year.
  4. List the doctors, specialists, and hospitals each person wants to keep seeing.
  5. List any regular prescriptions for everyone in the household.
  6. Think about care you expect next year, such as a planned surgery, a pregnancy, or ongoing therapy.
  7. Gather details about any job-based coverage someone in your home could get.

With this list in hand, you can compare plans based on your real life instead of guessing.

Here is a simple example. Imagine a couple who married this summer. One of them is self-employed, and the other just started a job that offers health benefits. Before choosing 2027 coverage, they might compare joining the employer plan together against keeping separate plans. They would check whether their doctors are in each network, look at the total cost of care they expect, not just the monthly premium, and think about how each plan handles a growing family down the road. There is no single right answer, but walking through it step by step makes the choice much clearer.

Mistakes to Avoid

Life changes can be busy and stressful, so a few things often slip through the cracks. Watch out for these:

  • Letting your plan renew automatically without checking whether it still fits your household.
  • Forgetting to update your income or household size on a Marketplace application.
  • Assuming a new baby or new spouse is added to your plan without you taking action.
  • Keeping a local-network plan after moving somewhere the network does not reach.
  • Waiting too long after a life event and missing a window to make changes.
  • Choosing a plan based only on the lowest monthly price without thinking about how your family will use care.

Avoiding these can save you headaches, unexpected bills, and gaps in coverage.

How a Licensed Advisor Can Help

Every household is different, and the rules around life changes can feel confusing. Prices, plan choices, financial help, and enrollment timing all vary by plan, state, and year. That is exactly where a licensed advisor can make things easier.

An advisor can look at your situation, explain whether a life event may let you make changes now, and help you compare both private PPO plans and ACA Marketplace plans side by side. They can help you check networks and plan details so you can make a choice with confidence. You still make the final decision, but you do not have to sort through it all on your own.
