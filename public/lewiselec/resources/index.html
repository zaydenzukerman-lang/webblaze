Getting to Know 3 Phase Motors

A short explanation of 3 Phase Motors

The Three Phase Motor is one of the most useful, and simplest, ways of converting electrical energy back into mechanical energy. The standard Wye (sometimes called "Star") wound motor consists of six (6) windings with nine (9) leads at the junction box. The Delta wound motor has the same number of windings with twelve (12) leads. Voltage, Frequency (Hertz), Frame type and Service factors change the construction of these motors but the basic operation remains the same.

The art of this is being able to understand what is connected to what and what should be connected to what under the given circumstances. The "is" are the windings themselves; they do not change. The "should" takes into consideration the voltage source and starter type (Wye/Delta, Part Winding, etc.) for the proper configuration of the windings.

Note that the Wye (Fig. A) has an internal winding connection for nine leads and the Delta (Fig. B) does not, so all twelve leads are available. Both are shown wired for their High Voltage (windings in series) to line voltage.

The pamphlet "Getting to Know 3 Phase Motors" shows how to:

  • Remember what numbers correspond to which windings.
  • Make High and Low Voltage connections for both the Wye and Delta configurations.
  • Obtain a different Voltage from a motor than the source Voltage for brake coils, etc.
  • Take a motor with no lead numbers and bring it back to life, step by step.
