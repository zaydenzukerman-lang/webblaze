Pools in Delray Beach

Older pools, a lot of them empty half the year

Delray pools skew older and smaller than the county average, and plenty of them sit on lots where the house went up in the fifties and the pool was added later. Tropic Isle and the beachside streets are close enough to the water that salt air is part of the maintenance picture.

A meaningful share of Delray homes are second homes, and that changes the job. A pool nobody is looking at for four months doesn't forgive a missed week, and it's exactly the situation where a service report earns its keep. You're reading it from Connecticut in February, and it's the only evidence you have that anyone is showing up. We treat seasonal accounts as full accounts, not as a reduced service that gets picked back up when you land.

Local know-how

What Delray pools need

1

Seasonal and absentee homes

Off-season is when a neglected pool turns. Seasonal pools stay on full weekly service and get the same report whether you're in town or not, so there's no surprise waiting in November.

2

Older surfaces

A lot of Delray pools are on marcite that's been patched more than once. Rough spots hold algae no matter how good the chemistry is, so we tell you when brushing has stopped being the answer.

3

Salt air, beachside

East of the Intracoastal, heaters and pumps corrode faster than the manual suggests. We check the pad for it rather than waiting for a failure in July.
