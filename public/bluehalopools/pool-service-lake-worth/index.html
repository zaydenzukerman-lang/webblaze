Pools in Lake Worth

Older pools, honest answers

Lake Worth has some of the oldest housing stock we service. College Park, the historic districts near Bryant Park and the streets off Dixie are full of small pools that were put in decades ago and have been maintained to wildly different standards since.

That means a lot of first visits here start with a real conversation about what the pool needs versus what it can get by on. Some of these pools genuinely need resurfacing and no amount of weekly chemistry will fix that. We'd rather tell you at the quote than take your money for a year and let you find out. When a pool only needs steady maintenance and a bit of patience, we say that too.

Local know-how

What Lake Worth pools need

1

Aging surfaces

Old plaster and marcite hold algae in the rough patches. We tell you plainly when brushing has stopped working and resurfacing is the actual fix, instead of selling more chemicals.

2

Undersized or tired equipment

Original pumps and filters are still running on plenty of these pools. We track filter pressure weekly so a failing filter is a planned replacement rather than a green pool.

3

Straight pricing

This is a price-conscious part of the county and we treat it that way. You get the rate before we start, and repairs are quoted before anything is ordered.

Pool service in Lake Worth

Questions

My plaster is rough and the pool keeps going green. Will weekly service fix it?

Sometimes, and sometimes not. Rough old plaster holds algae in the pitted areas no matter how good the chemistry is. We'll tell you at the quote which of the two you have.

How much is pool service in Lake Worth?

Plans start at $129 a month for weekly service on a standard residential pool, and every account is subject to a price quote by a technician or at the point of sale.
