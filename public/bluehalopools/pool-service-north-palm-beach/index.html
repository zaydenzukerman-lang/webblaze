Pools in North Palm Beach

Canals and canopy

Most of North Palm Beach went up in the fifties and sixties, and a great many homes back onto a canal. The tree canopy that grew in over the following seventy years is now the single biggest influence on the pools underneath it.

Heavy shade and constant leaf drop mean real chlorine demand and real skimming, and canal-side lots add wind-carried debris on top. The pools themselves are often original or resurfaced once, and they are usually in better condition than their age suggests because this is a village that maintains things. We would rather keep that going than find reasons to sell you work.

Local know-how

What North Palm pools need

1

Canopy and leaf load

Seventy years of tree growth over a sixty-year-old pool is a chlorine-demand problem before it is a skimming problem. It has to be dosed against, not just netted.

2

Canal-side debris and wind

Backing onto water adds debris and moves chemistry around. Baskets fill faster than the pool size would suggest.

3

Older pools in good order

Most of these have been looked after. The job is to keep them that way, not to find reasons to replace things.

Pool service in North Palm Beach

Questions

Our pool is under heavy tree cover. Does that change the price?

Usually a little. Constant leaf drop uses chlorine faster and adds time on site. On those pools the flat-rate chemical plan normally works out better than paying for chemicals as used.

We back onto a canal. Does that matter?

It adds wind-carried debris and moves the chemistry around more than an enclosed inland lot. Nothing unusual, just a reason to test rather than assume.
